Epigenetic heterogeneity in breast tumours informs diagnosis and prognosis.
Furth N, Riemenschneider C, Arieli Y, …, Shema E
▾

Abstract

Breast cancer, the most diagnosed cancer among women, is stratified by gene expression patterns into luminal and basal-like subtypes, and the latter is associated with worse prognosis1-3. Recent advances in single-cell technologies have underscored the presence of intratumour heterogeneity, which poses substantial challenges in cancer treatment and achieving long-term disease-free survival4-7. Here we extensively profile epigenetic heterogeneity in a cohort of treatment-naive human luminal tumours, which were obtained directly after surgical resection. By applying a single-cell epigenetic approach based on cytometry by time-of-flight, we identify a recurrent subpopulation of basal-like cells in luminal tumours with a conserved epigenetic signature across patients. These basal-like cells, also identified by deconvolution of whole-genome DNA methylation patterns, are characterized by high expression of aggressive oncogenic traits and downregulation of hormone receptor signalling. Direct comparison of single-cell proteomic and genomic profiling of monomethylation and trimethylation of histone 3 on lysine 4 revealed that these marks are key features associated with loss of luminal identity. Finally, we highlight the potential of concomitant single-molecule profiling of histone modifications on cell-free nucleosomes in blood as an epigenetic liquid biopsy approach for early diagnosis of breast cancer. Moreover, the epigenetic basal signature measured in blood is linked to prognosis, which enables the identification of patients at increased risk for disease relapse.

Transient nuclear softening for zygotic gene activation in mice.
Tanaka M, Sakanoue R, Miyagawa Y, …, Shimamoto Y
▾

Abstract

Nuclei in early mammalian embryos undergo epigenetic reprogramming that enables the zygote to develop into a complete organism. Whereas changes in chromatin state during this critical transition period have been extensively studied1-3, changes in the nucleus that houses chromatin remain poorly understood. Here, using direct organelle capture and biophysical manipulation methods, we find that nuclei attain profound softness and plasticity during the two-cell embryonic stage in mice, facilitating major zygotic gene activation. The prominent nuclear deformability is attributable to the rapid clearance of lamin B1 from the nuclear envelope by the autophagy factor LC3. Nuclear stiffening by lamin B1 retention or its overloading results in incomplete activation of zygotic genes and impaired development. However, embryos that maintain nuclear deformability escape from this transcriptional interference. High-resolution microscopy and sequential three-dimensional fluorescence in situ hybridization analyses revealed an accumulation of active RNA polymerase II and coordinated positioning of key genomic loci near the deformable nuclear envelope. An engineered molecular tether that stiffens the nuclei restricts these nuclear activities and suppresses expression of key zygotic gene activation genes. Together, our study identifies a distinct physical nuclear state that embryos acquire at the beginning of life that supports the initiation of the zygotic transcription program.

Multimodal single-cell epigenomic and 3D genome atlas of the human basal ganglia.
Ding W, Klein A, Báez-Becerra CT, …, Ecker JR
▾

Abstract

The basal ganglia (BG) underlie motor control, reward processing, and many neurological and psychiatric disorders, yet cell-type-resolved epigenomic and 3D genome maps of the human BG remain limited. Here, we present a multimodal single-cell atlas of DNA methylation (261,331 nuclei) and 3D chromatin conformation (229,286 nuclei) from eight subregions, harmonized under the BG consensus taxonomy and integrated with matched data across multiple regulatory layers. We identify millions of differentially methylated regions across cell types, enriched for distinct transcription factor motifs, and link them to disease-associated heritability. We characterize the POU6F2+ STR D2 SMYD2-HTR7 spiny projection neuron (SPN) subtype, with concordant signatures across multiple modalities, and reveal region-specific methylation and contact gradients. Integrative gene regulatory networks (GRNs) predict cell-type-resolved enhancer-promoter interactions, nominating candidate regulatory mechanisms for Parkinson's disease (PD) risk variants at SNCA. Finally, multiplexed error-robust fluorescence in situ hybridization (MERFISH) profiling combined with cross-species Patch-seq identifies a neuronal subtype, STR FS PTHLH-PVALB GABA, with electrophysiological differences across the striatal matrix-striosome (MS) boundary.

An integrative single-cell epigenomic atlas annotates the regulatory genome of the adult mouse brain.
Wang Z, Zu S, Armand EJ, …, Ren B
▾

Abstract

Histone modifications underpin the cell-type-specific gene regulatory programs that drive the remarkable cellular diversity of the mammalian brain. Here, we jointly profiled four histone modifications and the transcriptome in 2.5 million nuclei from the adult mouse brain. By integrating these data with chromatin accessibility, DNA methylation, and three-dimensional genome organization, we constructed a unified epigenomic atlas spanning over 100 brain cell subclasses and assigned active, primed, and repressive chromatin states across 81% of the genome. Active chromatin states marked by combinatorial histone modifications better nominate candidate enhancers with functional support than chromatin accessibility alone, while H3K27me3- and H3K9me3-marked chromatin states delineate distinct modes of cell-type-specific gene repression. Finally, this multimodal resource enables the development of deep learning models that predict cell-type-specific epigenomic features and gene expression from DNA sequence, providing a comprehensive framework for annotating the brain regulatory genome and interpreting non-coding disease risk variants.

Abstract

The basal ganglia are central to motor control, learning, emotion, and reward processing, and their dysfunction contributes to many neurological and psychiatric disorders. Here, we generated a single-cell multi-omic atlas of histone modifications and transcriptomes across eight basal ganglia regions from neurotypical human donors. This atlas resolved cell-type- and region-specific landscapes of active and repressive candidate cis-regulatory elements and delineated gene regulatory networks underlying cell identity. Integration with matched MERFISH spatial transcriptomics further uncovered regional heterogeneity in epigenomic and transcriptional programs. Finally, we used this atlas to interpret noncoding risk variants associated with neuropsychiatric disorders and to construct a deep-learning model that predicts gene regulatory activities and variant effects. Together, this work provides a multimodal regulatory framework for understanding human basal ganglia organization, evolution, and disease genetics.

Single-cell multimodal atlas reveals regulatory programs in the human brain.
Xie Y, Chang L, Zhong G, …, Ren B
▾

Abstract

Direct measurement of chromatin states together with transcription is essential for understanding how cell-type-specific regulatory programs are established and maintained in the adult human brain. Here, we present a large-scale single-cell multimodal atlas by jointly profiling the transcriptome with active (H3K27ac) or repressive (H3K27me3) histone modifications across 18 adult brain regions. We targeted over 750,000 nuclei spanning 160 cell types and integrated these data with chromatin accessibility, DNA methylation, 3D genome architecture, and spatial transcriptomes. This framework resolved chromatin states for over 500,000 candidate regulatory elements, identified enhancer-gene links and regulatory programs, revealed Polycomb-associated ultra-long-range contacts, and uncovered brainwide epigenomic heterogeneity. We further developed a sequence-to-function model to predict cell-type-specific effects of candidate neurological disease variants. Finally, comparative analyses across species reveal conservation in active regulatory programs but divergence in repressive programs. Together, this study provides a comprehensive epigenomic reference for interpreting regulatory principles and epigenetic memory underlying human brain organization and disease.

Circuit-aligned specialization of human basal ganglia astrocytes.
Fu Y, Kempynck N, Ding W, …, Bakken TE
▾

Abstract

Astrocytes shape synapses and circuits, yet their diversity within the human basal ganglia remains poorly defined. Integrating single-nucleus transcriptomic and epigenomic profiling with spatial transcriptomics, we identify three anatomically organized astrocyte subgroups-striatal gray matter (GM), extra-striatal GM, and white matter (WM)-distinguished by neurotransmitter transporter expression and synapse-associated programs that reflect afferent input. Abstract

Global upregulation of transcription, or hypertranscription, is observed in most human cancers, driven by the action of oncogene-encoded transcription factors (TFs). TF interactions with RNA Polymerase II (Pol II) are mediated by kinases that phosphorylate the Pol II C-terminal domain (CTD) to drive productive elongation. The combinatorial complexity of hundreds of transcription factors and kinases acting on thousands of genes that differ in expression between cell types obscures common features that may result from hypertranscription. Pol II profiling provides a direct measure of hypertranscription and histone gene upregulation in cancer, a pan-cancer diagnostic to stratify patient populations and follow the course of treatment. As many broadly effective anticancer drugs target Pol II activity, a better understanding of how they counteract hypertranscription has implications for drug development and for improved treatment regimens. Here we review the underlying mechanisms that drive hypertranscription and its impact on chromosome integrity, aging and neoplasia.

Abstract

PIWI-interacting RNAs (piRNAs) are small non-coding RNAs generated from longer precursor transcripts through primary processing and, in selected biological contexts, a secondary ping-pong amplification cycle. They associate with PIWI proteins to target and silence transposable elements (TEs), and dysregulation of the PIWI-piRNA pathway has been reported in multiple human cancers. In physiological germline models, epigenetic modifications - including DNA methylation, histone modifications, RNA modifications, and chromatin remodeling - coordinately regulate the activity of piRNA clusters to ensure proper piRNA biogenesis. However, in tumor cells, how epigenetic modifications influence piRNA transcription and processing remains poorly characterized, with most studies focusing on how dysregulated piRNAs drive tumorigenesis via epigenetic or epitranscriptomic mechanisms. This review also discusses species-specific differences, limitations in somatic piRNA annotation, and the evidence needed to distinguish association from mechanism. DNMT/HDAC inhibitors, dietary bioactive compounds, and deactivated Cas9 (dCas9)-based epigenome editing may provide useful experimental or therapeutic approaches, but piRNA-directed epigenetic therapy remains preclinical.
