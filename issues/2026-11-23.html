Evolutionary lineage-specific and heterochromatin-independent repression of transposon-derived sequences.
Matsushima W, Duc J, Sheppard S, …, Trono D
▾

Abstract

At least one-quarter of our gene regulatory sequences are derived from transposable elements (TEs). Their rapid evolutionary turnover fuels the innovation of species-specific regulatory networks, yet their activity must be tightly controlled for the integrity of broader gene regulatory programs. Although this is in part achieved through heterochromatin-based repression, notably by KRAB domain-containing zinc-finger proteins (KZFPs) in tetrapods, the regulatory landscape of TEs remains to be fully characterized. Here, we discover that SCAN domain-containing zinc-finger proteins (SZFPs), which originally arose through capture of the sequence encoding a retroviral capsid by a KZFP gene, have expanded their gene repertoires and diversified their DNA recognition specificity in a lineage-specific manner across amniotes. Our comprehensive identification of human SZFP binding sites revealed that they bind regulatory elements derived from distinct TE subfamilies of matching evolutionary ages. Mechanistically, we demonstrate that SZFP binding sites are inaccessible nucleosome-associated chromatin without accompanying heterochromatin marks, H3K9me3 or H3K27me3, and resist opening by pioneer transcription factors such as SOX2 and POU5F1 (also known as OCT4) as well as SWI/SNF-mediated gain of an enhancer-associated chromatin state. These results unravel a rapidly evolving epigenetic machinery responsible for controlling lineage-specific TE-derived regulatory sequences in a heterochromatin-independent fashion.

Tumor-intrinsic PTPN12 suppresses anti-tumor immunity in colorectal cancer by restraining a dual epigenetic and cytoskeletal dsRNA sensing program.
Huo Y, Tian Y, Wang D, …, Zhang Y
▾

Abstract

Innate sensing of endogenous double-stranded RNA and the ensuing interferon response are crucial for the efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibition. Microsatellite stable colorectal cancer characteristically lacks this response, remaining immune-refractory with undefined intrinsic regulators. Here, through an in vivo clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats screen, we identify PTPN12 as a physiological barrier that suppresses double-stranded RNA sensing and interferon response. TRIM28 was identified as a direct PTPN12 substrate here; PTPN12 removes phosphorylation at Y370 to stabilize repressive complexes and silence transposable elements, while concurrently rewiring the adhesive cytoskeleton to disrupt the spatially organization of cytosolic double-stranded RNA required for efficient innate sensing. Pharmacological inducible PTPN12 knockdown inhibits pre-established tumor growth and overcomes immunotherapy resistance in vivo, recapitulating PTPN12 ablation-induced massive double-stranded RNA accumulation. Our findings reveal a role for PTPN12 in coupling epigenetic silencing with cytoskeletal surveillance, suggesting potential strategies to augment anti-tumor immunity and overcome immunotherapy resistance in microsatellite stable colorectal cancer.

Circulating chromatin reveals effects of disease-associated variants on gene regulation.
Zhang Z, Chhetri SB, Jin Z, …, Baca SC
▾

Abstract

A fundamental challenge in human genetics is determining how regulatory genetic variation shapes complex traits and disease risk. Chromatin quantitative trait loci can link disease-associated variants to regulatory element activity, but mapping them in disease-relevant tissues at scale remains challenging. We leverage epigenomic liquid biopsy by profiling histone modifications in circulating chromatin from 328 cancer patients and 38 healthy individuals to identify cell-free chromatin quantitative trait loci. These loci affect regulatory element activities from diverse non-hematologic tissues and developmentally restricted elements reactivated in cancer. Through cistrome-wide association studies, we link 4891 loci to 1011 traits and diseases. Developmentally restricted loci absent in white blood cells are associated with 22.7 traits per 100 quantitative trait loci, compared to 0.58 for white-blood-cell-restricted chromatin quantitative trait loci. Here, we show that cell-free chromatin enables efficient and scalable discovery of regulatory genetic effects across tissues, diseases, and populations.

Somatostatin 6, a master maternal mRNA driving early development.
Chen J, Gao J, Nie T, …, Canario AVM
▾

Abstract

Vertebrate genomes contain six somatostatin (SST) gene family members, of which only SST1 and cortistatin (CORT or SST2) are present in mammalian genomes. These genes encode peptides that predominantly exert inhibitory effects on growth, metabolism, reproduction, and the immune system. However, their roles in nonmammalian vertebrates remain poorly understood. Here, we examined zebrafish sst6 (sst6.2) loss of function and discovered that maternally inherited sst6 mRNA is vital for embryonic cleavage and gastrulation. Its absence impaired calcium release from stores, calcium wave propagation, F-actin assembly, furrow ingression, and overall embryonic development. Moreover, maternal sst6 mRNA is crucial for degrading maternal mRNAs, for calcium-dependent phosphorylation of Stat3 and Smad1/5, and for activating zygotic gene transcription, including pou5f1, nanog, and sox2, as well as downstream pluripotency factors. This suggests that maternal sst6 mRNA acts as a master regulator of early development, a function likely conserved in other vertebrates.

Lactate-induced histone lactylation drives PRMT5-mediated Foxp3 symmetric dimethylation to promote Treg differentiation and sepsis immunopathology.
Wu D, Shi Y, Wang Y, …, Miao C
▾

Abstract

Sepsis is characterized by persistent immune dysfunction but how metabolic alterations contribute to immunosuppression remains unclear. Here, we identify lactate as a regulator of regulatory T cell (Treg) differentiation and show that Treg accumulation contributes to persistent organ injury during sepsis. In patients with sepsis, plasma lactate correlates with circulating Treg frequencies and disease severity. Lactate uptake through MCT1 increases H3K9 and H3K18 lactylation and Prmt5 transcription. Lactate also increases S-adenosylmethionine, supporting PRMT5-mediated symmetric dimethylation of Foxp3 at R51 and R146, which stabilizes Foxp3 and promotes Treg differentiation and suppressive function. CD4+ T cell-specific Prmt5 deletion attenuates lactate-induced Treg responses and improves outcomes in septic mice. Dexmedetomidine binds the PRMT5/MEP50 complex, reduces Treg accumulation and organ injury, and improves host defense against secondary infection. These findings link lactate metabolism to PRMT5-dependent Foxp3 stabilization and suggest a potential therapeutic pathway for sepsis-associated immunosuppression.

Abstract

Molecular analyses of brain regions implicated in risk are essential to providing insights into the antecedents and consequences of psychiatric disorder. In the current study, we contrast the genome-wide DNA methylation levels in the postmortem brains of individuals with a primary diagnosis of posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD; N = 101), major depressive disorder (MDD; N = 101), and controls (N = 100). We demonstrate: (1) the potential role of adult trauma in PTSD-associated oligodendrocyte reductions in the amygdala, (2) an association of MDD with differential methylation within the amygdala that may be accounted for by cell type enrichment, (3) sex- and diagnosis-specific multi-omics factors within the methylome that differentiate the immune and other biological pathways involved in PTSD, and (4) illustrate the lack of concordance in DNA methylation across brain regions in PTSD compared to MDD. We also find a brain-based biological aging factor that may provide insights into the biological pathways of brain aging.

Abstract

BCL11A is a pleiotropic transcription factor required for neurodevelopment, hematopoiesis, and the fetal-to-adult hemoglobin switch. Its erythroid-specific activity is a major therapeutic target for sickle cell disease and β-thalassemia. We investigate BCL11A function and regulation through three complementary approaches. First, we show that BCL11A ZF2-3 and ZF4-6 zinc-finger arrays recognize related 6-bp TGnCCA motifs through distinct strand-specific interactions, supporting a model in which BCL11A oligomers engage multiple DNA motifs. Third, screening 213 molecular glue degraders identifies compounds that promote degradation of the zinc-finger proteins IKZF1 and ZFP91 and induce HbF expression. Together, these findings define multiple regulatory mechanisms and complementary pharmacological strategies for HbF reactivation.

Abstract

In the Drosophila ovary, germline stem cells (GSCs) sustain oogenesis through a tightly regulated balance between self-renewal and differentiation. Although bag of marbles (bam) mutant ovaries have long been considered to predominantly consist of homogeneous GSC-like cells, the developmental states and regulatory programs underlying these undifferentiated germ cells remain poorly understood. Here, single-cell profiling of bam mutant ovaries uncovers distinct germ cell states, including two undifferentiated populations distinguished by eggpl expression and morphological features of bam mutant ovarioles. Comparative analyses further identify potential regulatory networks that are active at discrete developmental stages and reveal gcrf1 as a previously uncharacterized GSC-enriched gene. Functional studies demonstrate that gcrf1 promotes TORC1 signaling in early germ cells by activating the Rheb-S6K-S6 phosphorylation axis. Together, our findings reveal the developmental landscape of tumorigenesis caused by GSC differentiation defects and identify gcrf1 as a nutrient-responsive regulator linking TORC1 signaling to GSC maintenance.

多能性細胞は初期発生段階において連続的に存在し、あらゆる胚性系統へと分化する能力を持つことを特徴としています。この概念は当初、哺乳類の多能性幹細胞（PSC）のin vitroにおける挙動に基づいて定義されましたが、多能性は多様な細胞型を生み出すことで、動物界全体における複雑な体構造の構築に不可欠です。in vitroでは、PSCは胚性幹細胞として特定の状態に捕捉することも、細胞リプログラミングによって誘導多能性幹細胞（iPSC）を生成することもできます。これらのin vitroツールは、真獣類哺乳類モデルをはじめとする様々な動物における多能性の分子基盤を解明する上で中心的な役割を果たしてきました。本稿では、動物界全体における系統特異化という観点から、多能性の進化について考察します。まず、羊膜類と無羊膜類の分類群における、接合子から原腸形成までの多能性細胞集団の進行を概説し、これらの移行を発生砂時計モデルに照らし合わせて考察します。次に、中核となる多能性ネットワーク、すなわちPOU5-SOXB1-NANOG/VENTX自己制御ループが顎を持つ脊椎動物全体でどのように保存されているか、そして全ゲノム重複イベントがその構成要素のパラログ多様性をどのように形成したかを検討します。さらに、このネットワークの進化的な起源を考察し、自己複製と分化の間の拮抗関係は動物界全体で保存されている基本的な特性であり、それを制御する転写因子ネットワークは進化の過程で継続的に洗練されてきたことを示唆します。本レビューで提示する比較視点は、多能性の進化的な意義と、多様な種にわたる発生過程を制御する調節機構の両方を浮き彫りにします。
